# Storylines by presentation type

These are **starting structures, not mandatory sections**. Use them like this:
- **The user's structure wins.** If they supply an outline, keep its order and add only what they ask for.
- **Scale density to the slide count.** At about 2 minutes per slide, a 10-minute slot is roughly 5–6 slides plus the title.
- **Always open with the answer** (A2) unless the user asks for a build-up narrative.

Pattern codes refer to `slide-patterns.md`.

| Type | Default length | Density | Title style |
|---|---|---|---|
| Executive decision / board | 6–10 + appendix | Low on the slide, detail in the appendix | Action titles; decision stated on slide 2 |
| Strategy recommendation / business case | 10–15 + appendix | Medium | Action titles; one storyline device |
| Interview case presentation | 6–10 | Medium; show the reasoning | Action titles; assumptions explicit |
| Personal interview presentation | 5–8 | Low, with evidence-rich cards | Claim titles about fit and impact |
| Product demo / AI deployment proposal | 8–12 | Medium | User-problem framing → value → plan |
| Project update / implementation roadmap | 5–8 | Medium-high, tabular | Status-first titles |
| Custom | Ask or infer | n/a | n/a |

## Executive decision / board presentation
1. Cover (A1)
2. **Decision required + recommendation** (A2): what we are asking for, by when, and the headline impact
3. Context: why now (B1 or B6)
4. Options considered (C2), with trade-offs explicit
5. Recommended option: supporting evidence (B1/B7)
6. Financial impact: investment, return and payback (B5 bridge or B7 table, with sensitivities)
7. Risks and mitigations (D3)
8. Implementation and governance (D1)
9. **Decision required / next steps** (D5)
10. Appendix (D6): assumptions, sources, detailed financials

The board reads ahead, so each slide must stand alone. Put caveats in footnotes and keep the main-body text minimal.

## Strategy recommendation / business case
1. Cover
2. Executive summary (A2): recommendation + 3–4 governing messages
3. Situation and complication: market, customer or performance evidence (B1/B3/B4)
4. Diagnosis: root causes (C3 or B8)
5. Options and evaluation (C1/C2)
6. Recommendation detail: what changes (C5/C4)
7. Business case: value at stake (B6), financial bridge (B5), assumptions
8. Roadmap (D1)
9. Risks (D3) and KPIs (B6)
10. Next steps (D5)
11. Appendix

Repeat the governing messages word-for-word as section titles (MCK p4–8).

## Interview case presentation
1. Cover: state the answer in the headline
2. Executive summary (A2): answer + 3 reasons
3. Problem framing: the question, scope, success criteria, key assumptions
4. Approach / issue tree, briefly (C5 or a short process flow)
5. Analysis 1–2 (B1/B2/B7): each slide makes one claim; show your maths and label estimates
6. Options and recommendation (C1/C2)
7. Risks and mitigations (D3)
8. Implementation (D1) with success measures (B6)
9. Optional appendix: assumptions and calculations (D6)

Interviewers grade the reasoning. Make assumptions explicit with an `estimate` or `illustrative` tag, and keep any number the candidate could not defend out of the main body.

## Personal interview presentation
1. Cover: name, target role, one-line value proposition (user-provided)
2. Why this role / company: what the candidate understands about the company's challenges (sourced from official material)
3. Relevant experience (E1)
4. Evidence of impact (E2): 2–3 cases with results **exactly as the user stated them**
5. Role fit (E3)
6. Contribution plan: first 90 days (E4)
7. Close: what the candidate brings, plus questions for the panel

**Never invent experience, employers, metrics, titles or dates.** If information is missing, ask, or use clearly marked placeholders such as `[Result: add metric]`.

## Product demonstration / AI deployment proposal
1. Cover
2. Executive summary (A2): problem → solution → value → ask
3. User problem and current workflow (C3 with pain points)
4. Evidence of the problem's size (B1/B6)
5. Solution: how the workflow changes (C3 future state) and architecture (C4)
6. Demo storyboard or screenshots (user-supplied images only; label mock-ups)
7. Value: time, cost, quality and risk (B6/B5), with assumptions stated
8. Options or build/buy/partner (C2), if relevant
9. Implementation: pilot → scale with gates (D1)
10. Risks, governance and responsible AI (D3)
11. Success measures (B6) and next steps (D5)

For AI proposals, put human-in-the-loop, data access, accuracy measurement and change management on the slides. Executives expect most of the effort to be people and process (BCG p14–16).

## Project update / implementation roadmap
1. Cover
2. Status at a glance: overall RAG + 3 headlines + decisions needed (A2 with a status band)
3. Progress vs plan (D1 with a "today" line, or D2)
4. Milestones achieved / upcoming (B6 or table)
5. Risks and issues (D3)
6. Budget / resource status (B7)
7. Asks and next steps (D5)

## Custom type
Ask (or infer from the request) four things:
1. Who decides or judges?
2. What must they believe or do afterwards?
3. What evidence will convince them?
4. What constraints apply (time, confidentiality)?

Then build from the answer-first skeleton: answer → reasons → evidence per reason → risks → next steps, and pick patterns per slide by the job each slide does.
